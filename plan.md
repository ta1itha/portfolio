---

## The Core Mismatch (Read This First)

The portfolio is currently positioned for **Associate Technical Product Manager** roles. The Jem role is **Customer Success Manager** with 7–10 years of account management / customer success / implementation experience as the minimum bar.

You cannot pretend to be a 10-year CSM. The strategy is:
1. **Reframe** transferable assets in CSM language (relationships, onboarding, retention, adoption).
2. **Lean into the "Student Success Manager" role** — it is literally a success role with onboarding automation.
3. **Be honest** about being earlier in the CSM track but show you ramp faster than people who have only ever done CSM.

## Step-by-Step Plan

### Step 1 — Decide the positioning (do this before any edits)
The portfolio currently reads "developer turned educator turned product thinker." For Jem, reframe to:

> **"Educator turned product thinker turned customer partner — I turn complexity into outcomes for the people using the system."**

This keeps your authentic arc but lands on *customer* as the destination. Confirm this framing before touching the file.

### Step 2 — Update README.md positioning line
**File:** `README.md:3`
**Current:** `Personal portfolio website targeting Associate Technical Product Manager roles.`
**Change to:** `Personal portfolio website tailored for Customer Success roles in SaaS / HR tech.`

This is for recruiters / hiring managers who clone the repo or land on the GitHub page.

### Step 3 — Rewrite the Hero (index.html ~lines 49–80)
The hero is the first impression. Three edits:

1. **Hero eyebrow** (currently version-numbered): keep the `v2.0.0` motif but add a customer-anchor line, e.g. `v2.0.0 — building trusted partnerships with the people behind the product.`
2. **Hero tagline:** replace any PM-leaning sentence with one CSM-leaning sentence. Suggested:
   > *I work where customers, data, and complex systems meet — turning rollouts, change, and adoption into outcomes teams can feel.*
3. **CTAs:** keep `Download CV`. Change the secondary CTA from "View My Work" to **"See How I Work With People"** — anchors it to the Recommendations and Stroop sections.

### Step 4 — Reframe the "About / The Stack" section (index.html ~lines 83–132)
The opener "Some people follow a straight path. I've been pushing commits." is good — keep it. But the three mini-stats need to be CSM-flavoured:

| Current | Tailored for Jem |
|---|---|
| 700+ students mentored | **700+ users onboarded & supported** (educator = success at scale) |
| 3 internship case studies | **3 end-to-end implementation roadmaps delivered** |
| 90% code reduction | **107.9× ROI** modelled on the hospital scheduling case (commercial thinking) |

The "intersection" closing paragraph should land on *customer partnership*, not "product thinker."

### Step 5 — Reframe the `$ git log` version timeline (index.html ~lines 135–283)
Keep the format — it is distinctive and people remember it. But add a CSM lens to two entries:

- **v1.0.0 Noroff Educator** — add a line: *"Owned the relationship between curriculum, platform, and 700+ learners. Adoption, retention, escalations — the job under a different name."*
- **v1.2.0 Student Success Manager** — push this entry up visually. Rewrite the description to lead with: *"Customer success in education: onboarding, adoption, churn (drop-out) prevention, expansion (re-enrollment). Automated onboarding with PowerAutomate + Claude Code."*

This is the strongest single piece of CSM-relevant evidence you have. Do not let it sit in the middle.

### Step 6 — Repurpose one BA case study as an "implementation story"
The Stroop PM section (line 286) and BA presentations (line 386) are PM-coded. For CSM, add a short framing card *above* the BA carousel, something like:

> **// how I think about implementations**
> Each of these case studies is a customer-side implementation in miniature: discovery → stakeholder mapping → options analysis → phased rollout → measurable outcome. That's the same shape as onboarding a strategic Jem customer.

The Hospital WhatsApp case study is the best one to highlight for Jem — frontline workers, messaging tooling, behavioural change, hard ROI. Pin it first in the carousel order if it isn't already.

### Step 7 — Rework the Skills section for CSM keywords (index.html ~lines 482–567)
The current categories ("Product Management, Technical, AI & Automation, Tools & Platforms") signal PM. Re-bucket to:

- **Customer Success & Implementations** — onboarding, adoption, stakeholder management, MoSCoW prioritisation, change management, roadmap delivery
- **Commercial & Analytical** — ROI modelling, options analysis, KPI design, data-driven decision-making
- **Technical Fluency** — SaaS systems, API basics, automation (Claude Code, n8n, PowerAutomate), LMS / Zendesk / Confluence / JIRA
- **Communication & Enablement** — training & pedagogy (formal qualification), technical writing, remote-first collaboration

The point: every category name should be readable by a CSM hiring manager as "yes, I need that."

### Step 8 — Promote Recommendations higher up the page
Currently Recommendations sit at line 637 (near the bottom). For a relationship-driven role, **move this section to immediately after the Hero / About**. Trust signals are the single biggest thing a CSM hiring manager wants. Cut the section labels to one line and let the names + photos do the work.

### Step 9 — Add a short "Why Jem" block above Contact (new section)
A 4–5 line block that names Jem's mission back to them — frontline employees, financial wellbeing, scale-up energy. Example skeleton:

> **// why jem**
> Jem builds for the 200,000 frontline employees who usually get the worst version of every system. I have spent four years teaching people who get the worst version of every tool — I know what "simple, empowering" actually looks like when the user is tired, on shift, and on a 3G phone. That is the work I want to do next.

This single block does more than any keyword stuffing. Keep it under 80 words.

### Step 10 — Tailor the CV (assets/docs/t_obrien.pdf)
The portfolio is the funnel; the CV is what gets forwarded internally. Open `OBRIEN_CV.pdf` and check:

- Job title line on the CV — does it still say "Product Manager" or similar? Change the *headline* (not the work history) to something like **"Customer Success | Implementations | Onboarding at scale"**.
- Re-order the skills section so customer-facing skills sit above technical ones.
- Keep all dates and roles exactly as they are — never edit the truth, only the framing.

If the source is a Word/Pages file, edit that and re-export. If it is PDF-only, regenerate from the source.

### Step 11 — Write a Jem-specific cover letter
`COVER_LETTER.md` exists already. Either fork it to `COVER_LETTER_JEM.md` or overwrite it for this application. Must-include beats:

1. Open with **why Jem specifically** — name "frontline employees" and the 200,000 number. Recruiters notice.
2. Address the experience gap head-on in one honest sentence. ("I am earlier in the formal CSM track than your 7–10 year minimum, and here is what I bring that closes that gap faster than typical:")

4. End with one line on Nonsuku — read her LinkedIn (linked in the JD), find one thing that genuinely interests you, name it.

Keep it under 350 words.

### Step 12 — Domain proof: add a small "frontline / scale" callout
Anywhere in About or the new "Why Jem" block, work in this fact: you have personally supported users on slow connections, in different time zones, with varying tech literacy. Jem cares about this — most CSM candidates have only ever supported corporate office users.

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't strip out the developer / educator history.** Jem's JD literally says "exposure to project management" is a nice-to-have and that they value learning-obsession. Your range is an asset, not a liability — just point it at the customer.
- **Don't over-claim CSM years.** They will ask in the interview. The honest "I'm earlier in the formal title but here's why I close the gap" pitch beats puffery every time.
- **Don't keep PM-specific jargon** (roadmapping, backlog grooming, sprint planning) as primary descriptors. Use them as supporting evidence only.
- **Don't add a separate "Jem version" of the site** at a different URL. Keep one portfolio, tuned for this application. If you start branching, you'll forget which version is live.
